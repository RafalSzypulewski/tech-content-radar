This week's roundup is about the gap between what our tests say and what we can actually trust, especially as AI writes more of our code and our checks. It also looks at how to test AI models themselves, how the testing mindset has evolved, and how we talk about testing.

## When All Tests Pass and the Answer Is Still Wrong
A shop owner had AI write both a free-shipping rule and its tests, and both quietly assumed the same thing about whether the $50 threshold applied before or after coupons. Every test passed, yet the shop paid for shipping on discounted orders for over a month. It is a good reminder that when AI fills gaps in vague requirements, it can do so in the code and the tests alike, so green results do not equal confidence. The post is sponsored by SmartBear, and the author says the opinions are their own. [Original on The Weasel Speaks (Alan Page)](https://angryweasel.substack.com/p/green-tests-wrong-answers)

## AB Testing Episode 243: AI, Automation Limits and Cascading Failures
The hosts talk about AI-assisted software development, what automated testing can and cannot cover, and how failures cascade through complex cloud systems. The second half moves away from testing to voluntary retirement, financial certainty and finding purpose outside work. Testers will find the first half most relevant for thinking about risk in large systems. [Original on AB Testing (formerly Modern Testing; Alan Page, Brent Jensen)](https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/abtesting/episodes/Episode-243-e3sbphf)

## Testing AI Models as Magic Boxes
Michael Bolton shares an interview recorded at EuroSTAR 2026 for Richard Seidl's Software Testing Unleashed podcast. His argument is that a language model is a box nobody fully controls or understands, so testing it means exploring, experimenting and weighing risk rather than relying on scripted checks. This is useful framing for anyone being asked to test AI-based features. [Original on DevelopSense (Michael Bolton)](https://developsense.com/blog/2026/11/interview-on-software-testing-unleashed-podcast)

## Start Differently: The Story Behind Modern Testing
Alan Page explains how the AB Testing podcast, which began in 2014, grew into the Modern Testing principles, a way of describing software delivery in which quality involves much more than testing. He describes drafting the principles, reworking them on air with Brent Jensen, and continuing to iterate on them. He also mentions that a hike along the Pacific Crest Trail in 2025 pushed him toward writing a business fable on the topic.
