This roundup moves from the day-to-day craft of testing to the bigger questions AI is raising for our field. Two pieces look at the human side of QA work, namely career growth and communicating defects, while two from Martin Fowler's site examine how LLMs and coding agents are being perceived and why seasoned engineers remain cautious. The common thread: quality still depends on judgment, clear communication and proof that software actually works.

## Tester, QA Lead, Test Architect: Three Distinct Roles
This article treats the tester, QA lead and test architect as separate jobs and walks through how responsibilities around strategy, architecture and metrics shift as you move up. It also looks critically at what US Bureau of Labor Statistics figures can and cannot tell you about these career paths. Useful for anyone planning their next step in QA or trying to make sense of salary and job-market data. [Original on dev.to](https://dev.to/automationdatacamp/from-qa-tester-to-lead-qa-what-changes-and-what-us-data-cant-tell-you-oge)

## Writing Bug Reports Developers Appreciate
Drawing on five years in QA, including time leading test teams at a major Japanese testing company, the author lays out what turns a defect report into something developers find genuinely helpful. The focus is on structuring reports so issues can be reproduced and resolved quickly. A practical refresher on one of the most important and most underrated skills in testing. [Original on dev.to](https://dev.to/nagayashion/the-bug-report-your-developers-will-actually-thank-you-for-4kcp)

## Martin Fowler on the Widening Gap in How People See LLMs
Fowler picks up Andrej Karpathy's point that views of LLM capability vary enormously, from the public seeing a clumsy chatbot to a small group of practitioners seeing rapid, dramatic progress. He notes that free-tier tools still feel unreliable in his own use, and shares impressions from O'Reilly's Foo Camp, where attendees discussed immersion-style training for developers. A good reminder for testers to calibrate expectations of AI tools against what most users actually experience. [Original on Martin Fowler](https://martinfowler.com/fragments/2026-10-28.html)

## Why Experienced Developers Hesitate on Coding Agents
This piece argues that skilled developers' reluctance toward agentic development is less about loving to code or fearing for their jobs, and more about knowing that code which builds is not the same as code that runs reliably in production. The insight came from an open-space session during Thoughtworks' Technology Radar preparation and is tied to a culture that helped pioneer CI, automated testing and continuous delivery. For testers, it reinforces why verification and production confidence matter more than ever as AI generates more code. [Original on Martin Fowler](https://martinfowler.com/rachels-ramblings/good-devs-wary.html)
